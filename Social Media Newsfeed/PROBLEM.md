# LLD Machine Coding – Social Media Newsfeed (C++)

## Problem Statement

Design and implement a **Social Media Newsfeed** (like Twitter/X) supporting posts, follows, likes, comments, and a personalized feed generated by a **pluggable ranking strategy**, using Object-Oriented Programming in C++.

This problem is asked at Meta, Twitter, LinkedIn, and Sharechat interviews. It tests **entity modelling**, **fan-out vs pull feed generation trade-offs**, **heap-based top-K merging**, and the **Strategy pattern** for ranking.

---

## Functional Requirements

1. Users & social graph:
   - `createUser(userId, name)`, `follow(followerId, followeeId)`, `unfollow(followerId, followeeId)`.
2. Content:
   - `createPost(userId, text)` — timestamped post with unique id.
   - `likePost(userId, postId)` (a user likes a post at most once; unlike supported).
   - `commentOnPost(userId, postId, text)`.
   - `deletePost(userId, postId)` — only the author may delete; deleted posts vanish from all feeds.
3. Feed:
   - `getFeed(userId, count)` — top `count` posts from users that `userId` **follows** (self-posts included — document your choice), ranked by the active strategy.
   - **Ranking strategies** (selectable at construction):
     - `ChronologicalStrategy` — newest first.
     - `EngagementStrategy` — score = likes×2 + comments×3, tie-broken by recency.
   - **Pagination**: `getFeed(userId, count, offset)` or cursor-based — repeated calls page through the feed stably.
4. Queries:
   - A user's own posts (newest first); a post's like count and comments (oldest first).

---

## Business Rules

- Users cannot follow themselves; duplicate follows are no-ops.
- Liking your own post is allowed (document if you choose otherwise); liking twice is a no-op.
- Feed must NOT contain posts from unfollowed users — unfollowing takes effect on the next feed read.
- Deleted posts must not appear anywhere, and their likes/comments are gone with them.
- A user who follows nobody sees only their own posts (or an empty feed — match your self-post choice).
- All timestamps come from an injected clock for deterministic ordering in tests.

---

## Non-Functional Requirements

- In-memory only.
- Standard C++ library only (`unordered_map`, `set`, `priority_queue`, etc.).
- Follow OOP principles; ranking must be swappable without touching feed assembly.
- `getFeed` should avoid scanning **all posts in the system** — gather candidates from followees, then top-K with a heap.
- No UI; demonstrate via `main()`.

---

## Design Expectations (What Interviewers Look For)

### 1. Class Modelling

Expected core classes:
- `User` (id, name, followees set, followers set)
- `Post` (id, authorId, text, timestamp, likes set, comments list, deleted flag)
- `Comment` (id, authorId, text, timestamp)
- `SocialGraph` (follow/unfollow, followees lookup)
- `FeedRankingStrategy` (interface: score/compare posts) + `ChronologicalStrategy`, `EngagementStrategy`
- `NewsfeedService` (public API, feed assembly, pagination)

---

### 2. Feed Assembly (Pull Model)
- Collect posts of all followees, push into a bounded heap for top-K by the strategy comparator.
- Complexity: O(P log K) over candidate posts P — be ready to state it.
- Discuss the **push (fan-out-on-write) vs pull (fan-in-on-read)** trade-off even though pull is implemented.

---

### 3. Strategy Pattern
- The feed code depends only on the strategy interface; adding a "trending in last hour" strategy requires zero changes elsewhere.

---

### 4. Consistency Details Interviewers Probe
- Stable pagination when posts arrive between pages (define the behaviour: snapshot cursor vs best-effort).
- Unlike/redundant like idempotency.
- Delete visibility in already-computed structures.

---

## Constraints

- Single-threaded; no real-time push delivery.
- No media, hashtags, or search in core.
- Follower counts are small enough for pull-model feeds; no sharding needed.
- No persistence.

---

## Example Scenario

```text
createUser(u1..u4); u1 follows u2, u3
t=1 u2 posts P1 "hello"
t=2 u3 posts P2 "world"
t=3 u4 posts P3 "hidden"          (u1 doesn't follow u4)

u4 likes P1; u3 likes P1; u1 comments on P2

getFeed(u1, 10) [Chronological] → [P2, P1]        (P3 excluded)
getFeed(u1, 10) [Engagement]    → [P1(score 4), P2(score 3)]

u1 unfollows u3 → getFeed(u1, 10) → [P1]
u2 deletes P1  → getFeed(u1, 10) → []

getFeed(u1, 1) then next page → returns remaining posts without duplicates
```
